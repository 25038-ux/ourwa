# 0. EXECUTIVE DIRECTIVE TO CLAUDE

You are not being asked to make a normal CRUD SaaS.

You are the principal architect and senior engineering lead for
**FORSA**, a potentially venture-scale company.

Your job is to build a system that can begin in Mauritania but is
architected from day one so that its core intelligence layer can scale
internationally.

Do not confuse the initial market with the ultimate architecture.

The first wedge is:

> **Help Mauritanian companies discover the opportunities they are most
> capable of winning, understand the requirements and risks, prepare
> compliant responses, find partners when they lack capabilities, and
> continuously learn from outcomes.**

The platform must eventually evolve into:

> **A commercial intelligence operating system for companies.**

The product should ingest fragmented public and authorized data,
normalize it, understand it, connect it to a structured company
capability graph, generate evidence-backed recommendations, and turn
recommendations into executable workflows.

The product must NOT be:

-   a simple tender scraper;
-   a keyword search engine;
-   a generic chatbot;
-   a generic RAG application;
-   a generic AI proposal writer;
-   an AI wrapper around an LLM;
-   an ordinary dashboard;
-   a system that invents eligibility or business facts;
-   a system that blindly scrapes sites in violation of terms or
    technical controls.

The system must be:

-   evidence-first;
-   source-aware;
-   multilingual;
-   explainable;
-   auditable;
-   secure;
-   modular;
-   observable;
-   testable;
-   resilient to source changes;
-   designed for human approval where decisions have legal/commercial
    consequences;
-   built around a canonical data model rather than around one website;
-   capable of learning from company outcomes without turning
    predictions into facts.

# 1. RESEARCH CONTEXT AND COMPETITIVE LESSONS

Before implementing the system, understand what already exists.

The research phase found several important categories of systems:

### Tender/procurement intelligence

-   **Tenderlake** demonstrates capability-based matching rather than
    relying only on keywords, with scored and explained opportunities
    and risk flags.
-   **Tendly** combines tender discovery, company profiles, matching and
    tender analysis.
-   **Deltek GovWin IQ** demonstrates the importance of early
    procurement intelligence, historical data, analyst validation, buyer
    intelligence and pre-RFP signals.
-   **Tendly/TenderRender/Lucius/TenderAI/BidDNA** demonstrate that the
    market is moving toward structured bid/no-bid analysis, compliance
    matrices, risk scoring and AI-assisted response generation.
-   **Loopio** demonstrates the value of a trusted content library,
    proposal workspaces, response workflows and evidence-backed reuse.

### Critical lesson

Do NOT attempt to win by cloning these products.

The opportunity is to combine their strongest patterns with a
**Mauritania-first data advantage, multilingual intelligence layer,
local business graph, local procurement context, partner discovery and
emerging-market workflow design**.

The long-term moat should not be "we have an LLM."

The moat should be:

1.  proprietary normalized opportunity history;
2.  company capability graph;
3.  buyer/authority graph;
4.  supplier/partner graph;
5.  historical awards and outcomes;
6.  document/evidence graph;
7.  local terminology and multilingual normalization;
8.  learned bid/no-bid outcomes;
9.  procurement-plan-to-tender linkage;
10. high-quality source lineage;
11. workflow data generated by actual users.

# 2. CURRENT MARKET FACTS TO DESIGN AROUND

The official Mauritanian National Public Procurement Portal currently
exposes procurement information and documents, including current notices
and annual procurement plans.

The portal currently reports thousands of records across categories such
as:

-   Fournitures
-   Prestations intellectuelles
-   Travaux
-   Services courants

It also provides annual procurement plans and email notification
functionality.

Important: **the existence of official alerts means FORSA must not
compete merely on "we notify you when a tender appears."**

The real value is:

> **"We determine which opportunities matter to YOUR company, why, what
> could disqualify you, what evidence proves your fit, what you are
> missing, how much effort it will take, whether you should bid, and
> what to do next."**

The portal also exposes procurement plans containing future activities,
which creates an important pre-opportunity intelligence layer.

UNGM exposes developer APIs for notices, vendors, awards, expressions of
interest and related procurement data. Where authorized and technically
permitted, FORSA should prefer official APIs over scraping.

World Bank procurement data is publicly available through its
procurement notices ecosystem. Similar official/authorized sources
should be prioritized.

Mauritania's digital-economy trajectory also supports the thesis:
internet adoption is high relative to the country's size, while SME
technology adoption, digital skills, informality and fragmented business
processes remain important constraints.

# 12. MATCHING ENGINE

Do not use one LLM prompt.

Use a multi-stage architecture.

## Stage A --- deterministic eligibility gates

Check:

-   mandatory legal status;
-   geography;
-   deadline;
-   required certification;
-   minimum experience;
-   financial thresholds;
-   registration;
-   procurement method;
-   nationality/residency requirements;
-   consortium requirements.

## Stage B --- semantic capability matching

Embeddings + ontology.

## Stage C --- evidence validation

Map company claims to evidence.

## Stage D --- capacity assessment

Check:

-   staff;
-   workload;
-   project size;
-   timeline;
-   geography.

## Stage E --- strategic fit

Consider:

-   buyer relevance;
-   sector strategy;
-   previous relationships;
-   geographic expansion;
-   future market value.

## Stage F --- bid economics

Estimate:

-   likely effort;
-   proposal cost;
-   expected contract value;
-   gross-margin assumptions if provided;
-   expected value;
-   capacity opportunity cost.

## Stage G --- AI explanation

Generate a human-readable explanation from structured signals.

# 14. BID / NO-BID ENGINE

Create explicit decision gates.

Example:

### Automatic NO-BID

If:

-   mandatory certification absent;
-   mandatory eligibility condition impossible;
-   deadline impossible;
-   company explicitly excludes the service;
-   legal restriction blocks participation.

### BID WITH CONDITIONS

If:

-   one missing item can be obtained;
-   consortium solves a gap;
-   supplier partnership is required;
-   financial capacity needs confirmation.

### REVIEW

If:

-   evidence incomplete;
-   ambiguous requirement;
-   contradictory source documents.

### BID

If:

-   hard gates pass;
-   capability is strong;
-   capacity is feasible;
-   economics are reasonable.

Never make an irreversible decision automatically.
